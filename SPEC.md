SF Live Suite — Spec v2
Nov 3, 2026 · Wiley
Source of truth. Decisions are dated inline. Builds are tested as whole suites only.

# Purpose

A suite of Ableton Live plugins (AU + VST3, macOS, Apple Silicon) for performing hypnotic / dub techno live: very simple note content (often one note, fixed patterns), with all variation played on knobs. Designed from the start for a fixed live rig: one Live set that never changes, songs switched by preset, the OXI One MkII sequencing, the OXI E16 knob array (absolute 0-127 CC with value feedback) playing.

The releases are live sets, not tracks [Nov 3]: sets are recorded as played and released, from a bedroom session to a festival main stage. So the bar is release-grade sound from every plugin at 44.1 / 48 / 96 kHz and any buffer size, and nothing a performer does with a knob may produce a click, an alias, an inter-sample over, a level jump or a dropout. See Sound quality.

# Standards

## Visual
Black, white and greys. Colour only carries information:
- RED: clip / fault / missing file
- AMBER: conflict / warning / armed-but-waiting states that need attention
- GREEN: active / locked to the grid / source connected / gliding: the E16's own ring green (#4cf59b, a mint; it was #30d158 before Nov 6), so a gliding knob on screen matches its ring
- CYAN: the value of the control being touched

Monospace type, 1 px rules, no decoration, no gradients, no shadows. One exception: the E16's encoders are drawn as the hardware (knurled black caps, 16 LEDs round each, lit in the scene's ring colours: white, LIGHT BLUE for a volume, green for SLOW ALL and a glide), so a knob on screen looks like the one under the hand. [Nov 6]

The window [Nov 6] (Source/core/Editor.h): one narrow strip, 296 px (four 70 px columns, the E16's 4 x 4), so several sit side by side; top to bottom from what is played to what is set once:
- HEADER: SF <MODULE>, SONG, OUT meter (FAULT / PANIC); PRESET, STORE, LOAD.
- E16: the plugin's page of the scene SF SHOW (E16Map.h's kE16Pages, written by controllers/e16/make_scene.py from layout.py) as a 4 x 4 grid: the page with most of the plugin's encoders 9-16 (the first page if it is only on 1-8, which every page shares), its own turns lit where they sit (a turn's second destination too: SF CONTROL's SLOW ALL on encoder 1, green) and the other encoders dark, their E16 names faint. The plugin's own track is found as GLIDE RINGS finds it (E16View.h: Live's track name; a plugin on one track of the scene without it; on several and unnamed, as an AU always is: no grid, a line saying so, its knobs in PERFORM). A press (THROW, FREEZE, DROP, ROLL) sits under its knob: hold for a hold, click for a toggle. A ring fills as the E16's does (from the LED just left of the bottom, clockwise; the last LED partly), up to the top of Live's mapping (DUB FEEDBACK: full at 70 %). The line under the grid says where else the plugin is (ALSO ON PERFORM 9) or that encoders 1-8 are on every page.
- PERFORM: the rest of the top row and what else is played though not in it (ParamSpec::perform: DECK's PLAY and CUE, CONTROL's PANIC); a plugin not on the E16 starts here.
- The module's display. A sampler with a sample loaded shows it at the display's foot (a strip 50 px high; PERC, which has no display of its own, shows only it) [Nov 6]: the waveform, the part that plays bright and the rest dim, the fades as ramps, START and END as lines with a tab. VOICE draws the same over its phrase.
- DETAIL: the other sound controls; MODEL first, then knobs, menus, switches, each in the module's order (a menu with long choices takes two columns; a row's free column takes the next cell that fits).
- SAMPLE (the samplers; ParamSpec::inSample): START, END, FADE IN, FADE OUT [Nov 6]. Folded at first, like the groups below.
- MOTION, SYSTEM (SLOW, MACRO SRC, MACRO AMT) and SETUP (ParamSpec::setup, set and forget: ALLOC, KEY FOLLOW, DRY, IMPORT, ROLE, SOURCE, PC CHANNEL ...): folded at first, each title line saying what is set (M1 SINE>CUTOFF 1 BAR  M2 OFF). A click on a title line opens or folds the group (DETAIL too); the plugin saves which differ from the defaults with the set (the state's "ui" property, only when not the defaults; older builds ignore it).
Every knob is drawn as an encoder: green while SLOW moves it (the sound's place, the ringed LED where it goes), a green dot outside the ring where MACRO / MOTION have it, the value cyan while touched; a knob whose middle is neutral (BIP, SLOT, a range either side of 0) fills from the top either way, except in the E16 grid. The window repaints a control only when what it shows changes. Test: SFTests --editor (every module on no name, an unknown name and each of its scene tracks: every parameter shown once, hidden ones never; each of the scene's encoders for that track at its place on the page, presses under them; the tallest strip at first under 900 px (PAD, 874), the window the whole strip when the screen holds it; folds saved with the set and nothing saved for the defaults). [93d84a4 audit, B05] The window is never taller than the screen it is on allows (heightLimit: that screen's usable height less 64 px for Live's title bar and a margin; the main screen's until the window shows; checked again every second, so a window moved to a smaller screen fits it): under the header the strip (the E16 grid, the groups, the display) is a scrolling view, its bar 6 px wide over the right margin, shown only when needed. While the strip scrolls the mouse wheel scrolls it and turns no knob (a knob under the pointer would take it); when it fits, the wheel turns knobs as before. A group opened below the window scrolls into view, its title first. All groups open, PAD is 1148 px. SFTests --reach: all 32 strips with every group open, held to 480, 640 and 800 px: no taller, and every control, the display and every title line scrolled whole into view (a display taller than the window, ALLOC's at 480: its top, then its bottom), the bottom of the strip reached; the wheel over a knob scrolls (66 strips) and turns none, and turns it when the strip fits; a set saved with every group open reopens held to a 640 px screen, open, at the top; SETUP opened at the bottom scrolls into view; PANIC and THROW held as the window closes are let go, scrolled or not.

## Naming
Boring, practical, uppercase. Plugins appear in Live as `SF <ROLE>`. Parameters are plain words (`DENSITY`, `GATE`, `CUTOFF`, `DEPTH`). Models are descriptive (`KICK 909-TYPE`, `ACID 303-TYPE`, `STAB DUB`). Trademarked machine names appear only as "-TYPE" descriptions. Presets are `GROUP/NAME` in the same plain words (`DUB/CHORD 1`, `ACID/SQUELCH`); no artist names, no trademarks.

## Controls
- The first 8 parameters of every plugin are its performance row (Live's device view, one E16 page). The window groups the rest by how often a hand goes there (Visual: the window).
- Every knob is smoothed (30 ms) so 7-bit CC never steps audibly; ranges and curves are tuned so the whole travel is usable.
- Standard trio wherever it applies: DENSITY (how many events), GATE (how long each lasts), FILTER (bipolar DJ filter: left low-pass, centre open, right high-pass).
- System controls on every plugin (appended, never in the first 8):
  - SLOW: CONTROL / 1-64 BAR / OFF / 128 BAR (appended after OFF [Nov 5], so saved sets keep their settings). Every performance-row knob travels to where it is set over exactly that many bars, however far it moved (with SLOW at 32 BAR a quarter turn becomes a 32-bar rise). Song recalls glide the same way. MACRO moves stay immediate. While travelling, the knob's ring is green and walks with the sound, the ringed LED is the target, the value is green (it showed "GLIDE now>target" before the Nov 6 window). [decided Nov 2: fixed time, performance row only]
    CONTROL (the default; it was OFF, at the same index, so older sets sound the same while SLOW ALL is OFF) takes the length from SF CONTROL's SLOW ALL (OFF / 1-128 BAR; 128 BAR [Nov 5]: about four minutes at 132 BPM, for long sets), one knob for the whole set, honoured only while CONTROL runs. A length or OFF (appended, so saved lengths keep their index) is the plugin's own and wins over SLOW ALL. The length is fixed when the move is made (a turn, automation, a recall): changing SLOW or SLOW ALL never touches a knob already travelling, it only sets how the next moves go; a knob moved again while travelling sets off from where it got to. [decided Nov 3: SLOW ALL, so a move can be a glide or direct without opening a plugin] [Nov 5 audit, F9] SLOW reaches the performance row and the detail knobs marked slow() in a module's parameter list (ParamSpec::slows; for now the instruments' COLOR, DETUNE, ATTACK, SPACE, which the E16 has as TEXTURE COLOR / DETUNE / ATTACK and STAB SPACE): where SLOW reaches is not where a knob is drawn, and no parameter moved to make it glide. Other knobs follow in ~30 ms; menus and switches change at once (MODEL, CHORD, ECHO SPACE, a dropped sample); Live's faders never glide. A song recall with SLOW is a morph of the knobs, not a crossfade between two whole sounds. The operator's manual's WHAT SLOW MOVES table and SFTests --scope come from the same sources (layout.py, specs.json's "slow"; kE16Knobs). [Nov 5 audit, F7] SLOW ALL changed in the same host block as a move: the move takes the length from before the change, in every plugin, whatever order Live runs the tracks in. CONTROL publishes on its last chunk the old value, the new one and the song position where its host block ends (bus v8, a sequence lock); a plugin whose block starts before that point (within 2 blocks) while playing uses the old value. With the transport stopped there is no shared position: the new value counts at once (a plugin Live runs before CONTROL in that one block still has the old one). [352df3c audit, A05] The fence is used once: CONTROL takes it down in its next block, and a plugin that has played past the change's position (or run with the transport stopped) never uses the old value for that change again (slowPassed), so a loop or a jump back to that position uses SLOW ALL as it is (it used to bring the old value back there).
  - MACRO SRC (OFF, MACRO 1-6) and MACRO AMT (-100..+100 %): the plugin follows one of SF CONTROL's six macros. Each module decides which knobs respond and in which direction.
  - MOTION [added Nov 3]: two modulators, M1 and M2, each SOURCE / RATE / TARGET / DEPTH. See MOTION below.
- PRESET menu in every header [added Nov 3]: factory presets (191 across 26 plugins) and user presets. See Presets below.
- Nothing can hurt the PA: resonance is level-compensated (and a resonance guard rides peaks above 0.9 down with a smooth gain, never a waveshaper), feedback above 100 % saturates instead of exploding, every output is clamped at +12 dBFS. NaN/Inf, or 250 ms held at the clamp (a runaway), replaces the output with silence and resets the module; the header's meter label shows FAULT in red. PANIC on SF CONTROL silences every SF plugin (see Engine).

## Live / controller workflow (decided Nov 2)
- E16 is mapped with Live's MIDI Map (Cmd-M) with Remote on for its output, so knob rings follow song and value changes. The plugins report every recalled value back to Live.
- Song change: Program Change on MIDI channel 16 into SF CONTROL (channel selectable). Recall lands on the next bar line while Live plays, at once when stopped. VST3: SF CONTROL exposes 16 programs (SONG 01-16); Live 12.2 and later deliver MIDI Program Change to VST3 plugins as a program change (12.2b10 release notes), earlier versions don't; the AU reads Program Change from MIDI in any version. [decided Nov 2, updated Nov 3; needs Live test]
- Stems from Live's own stem separation go on their own tracks; SF TAP on each puts them into Pocket and ALLOC.

# Sound quality [decided Nov 3]
Nothing audible may come from the implementation. Measured by SFTests (`--quality`), every build.
- Oversampling: every nonlinearity runs oversampled.
  - Polyphase IIR halfbands (elliptic, HIIR-style design, cascaded for 4x): flat to 20 kHz within 0.0001 dB, image and alias rejection 118 dB at 44.1 kHz, 112 dB at 48 kHz, 123 dB at 96 kHz. Near-zero latency, so it is used inside voices and feedback loops.
  - Linear-phase FIR (79- and 23-tap halfbands, then a Kaiser low-pass decimator, 100 dB) where phase matters: SF LIMIT's clipper. 99.5 dB rejection.
  - Rates: synth voices 2x (ladder, MS20-TYPE and SYNC models 4x); KICK, PERC 2x; DRIVE and STRIP drive 4x (DRIVE moved from JUCE's oversampler, about 70 dB, to the suite's own); FILTER, ENERGY, VOICE drive 2x; DELAY loop saturation 2x; RUMBLE grit 2x; LOWEND sub clip 2x; STRIKE fracture 2x; TONE 2x; LIMIT clip 4x linear phase.
  - Oversampled stages are never switched in and out (that would move their delay and click): they run all the time, and each drive fades in from exactly clean at 0 (FILTER, ENERGY, STRIP across the whole knob; synth DRIVE over the first 10 %; VOICE DRIVE and RUMBLE GRIT over the first 2 %, so their calibrated defaults are unchanged). [review, Nov 3]
  - LIMIT's FIR stages are sized from the sample rate (2x stage 151 taps below 46 kHz, 79 above; 4x stage 31 taps): the whole path is flat to 20 kHz within 0.01 dB at 44.1, 48 and 96 kHz.
- Oscillators: phases wrap for any increment and partials above 0.45 x the voice rate go silent (TUNE +24 on note 127 used to read past the end of a table) [Nov 3, second audit]; band-limited wavetables (one table per octave, harmonics kept below 0.75 x the oversampled Nyquist, 4-point Hermite reads), hard sync with BLEP correction, polyBLEP carriers in VOICE, first-order anti-derivative anti-aliasing (ADAA) on tanh stages. Alias floor, worst case across a 3-octave sweep: ACID 303-TYPE 94 dB, FM 97 dB, STRING MACHINE 105 dB, SUB OSC 94 dB, MS20-TYPE 77 dB, SYNC 79 dB.
- Samples: files are converted to the session rate with a windowed-sinc resampler on load. Playback reads at or below the original rate with 4-point Hermite; read faster (transposed up), it uses a Kaiser-windowed sinc low-pass at 0.9 / rate, so nothing folds back (a 17 kHz sample an octave up used to alias to 14 kHz at full level; now -107 dB). Above twice the original rate it reads an octave-down copy made at load (half-band filtered, flat to 0.43 of the band, 90 dB down from 0.58) where the rate is between 1 and 2, so the cost stays at most about 36 taps however far up a sample is played (the plain reader costs about 18 x rate taps). Every sampler and DECK's varispeed share the reader. [octave-down copies: Nov 3, second review] Floating-point files that peak above full scale are stored scaled with their gain, and reopen unchanged. [Nov 3, second audit]
- Engine: audio runs in 64-sample chunks on a fixed grid of the stream (see Engine), so the output does not depend on Live's buffer size.
- SF LIMIT: see Master / mix. True peak at the output never exceeds CEILING (8x detection, measured on dense clipped material, sine sweeps and square waves).
- Loudness metering (LIMIT, DECK analysis): ITU-R BS.1770-4 / EBU R128. K-weighting designed exactly at any sample rate; momentary 400 ms, short-term 3 s, integrated with absolute (-70 LUFS) and relative (-10 LU) gating on a 0.1 dB histogram (constant memory over a whole set). Reads -20.00 LUFS on the 1 kHz reference sine.
- REVERB: feedback delay network with input diffusion (four all-passes per channel) and two-band absorption in every line (shelving: DARK shortens the highs while the lows keep DECAY). RT60 of the lows at DECAY 1 / 3 / 8 s: 0.99 / 3.04 / 8.00 s. Line lengths, loop gains, absorption and modulation depth ramp across each chunk and glide (30 ms), so SIZE / DECAY / DARK / MOD under a knob, automation, MACRO or MOTION never zipper. Reverbs inside ENERGY and STRIP ring out after their send closes instead of being cut.
- DELAY: TONE and saturation sit inside the feedback loop, so each repeat is darker (or thinner) and dirtier than the last. FREEZE crossfades (10 ms) into a lossless loop (feedback 1, no filtering, the wow stops, the head reads whole samples once it has settled; TIME turned while frozen reads between samples until it settles, so it bends instead of zippering) [second review]. REVERB FREEZE fades the input into the frozen space over 10 ms (cutting it would circulate the cut for the whole hold) [second review].
- GLUE: 6 dB soft knee.

## Gain staging [decided Nov 3]
Every instrument model is trimmed so that at default settings it plays at its role's loudness target (integrated LUFS, role pattern): KICK -15, PERC -22, RUMBLE -20, BASS -18, LEAD -20, PAD -22, VOICE -20, STRIKE -20. Switching MODEL never jumps the level, and a rig at default LEVELs is balanced with headroom on the master before GLUE and LIMIT. Trims are in `Source/modules/Calibration.h`, applied after any drive, and verified by SFTests (`--calibration`). Sample models are not trimmed (the file's own level counts).

# Shared architecture

## SF BUS
One block of POSIX shared memory (`/sf_live_suite_bus_v11`) mapped by every SF plugin, across plugin binaries and processes. Lock-free atomics only. A segment of another version or layout (another build; the layout size is stored in the segment, since macOS rounds the segment size up to whole pages) is never mapped; that plugin falls back to a local bus, so all plugins in a set must come from the same build. Verified Nov 2: two separately built plugin binaries (SF KICK, SF DUCK) loaded in a VST3 host share the bus and Pocket works between them. Carries: macros, song select / store requests, PANIC, KEY, SLOW ALL, glide reports, kick reports, ALLOC source energies and cuts, SF TERROIR's capture requests and SF CAPTURE's reports. v3 [Nov 3] added PANIC and KEY; v4 [Nov 3, second audit] added the recall bar line, held PANIC and source ids; v5 [Nov 3, third review] added SF BUILD's drop (bar line, depth, duck window, where and when it was planned), STORE TO's song, and where a song change was asked for. v6 [Nov 3] added SF CONTROL's SLOW ALL; v7 [Nov 4] the glide reports (GLIDE RINGS); v8 [Nov 5] SLOW ALL's previous value and the song position from which its new value counts (F7); v9 [Nov 5] the in-set capture (SF TERROIR asks, SF CAPTURE answers: request, claim, cancel, state, error, progress, peak, heartbeat); v10 [Nov 5, audit of 352df3c] the capture's status as one word (the request's number, its state, its error), the claimant's lease, and the number of the request a STOP cancelled; v11 [Nov 5] SLOW ALL's 128 BAR (a plugin from before it would take it for 64). If shared memory is unavailable the bus falls back to process-local (plugins in one process still talk); SF CONTROL shows BUS SHARED / LOCAL ONLY.

## Clock
Live's transport when playing. When stopped, a wall clock at the last tempo stands in, so tempo-synced motion keeps running and every SF plugin agrees on the beat.

## Engine [decided Nov 3]
- Every plugin processes in 64-sample chunks on a fixed grid of the audio stream, whatever Live's buffer size. Parameters (30 ms smoothing, SLOW glides), MACRO and MOTION update once per chunk at the same stream positions every time, and envelopes, modulators and Pocket run on that grid, so the output is the same at any buffer size: bit-identical at buffer sizes 1 to 2048 for every module (SFTests). MIDI events keep their exact sample offsets inside the chunk.
- Song recall lands on the exact sample of the bar line (the chunk is cut there), not on the next buffer. SF CONTROL decides the bar line once and sends it with the song (bus v4): every plugin lands on the same bar whatever order Live processes the tracks in (a plugin processed before CONTROL in that audio cycle sees the change a block later and catches up at once, on the same bar). A change requested within 30 ms after a bar line (a Program Change sent on the downbeat) belongs to that bar; later in the bar, to the next one. Positions are compared with a 0.001-sample tolerance, so rounding of the song position can never push a recall to the following bar. [Nov 3, second audit]
- After a recall the recalled values hold until Live reports them back; the moment the host's value moves away from what it was at the recall (the performer turns the knob, or automation), it has the parameter. STORE ALL in that window stores the recalled values. [Nov 3, second audit / second review] A value Live still disagrees with 3 s after it was told gives way; the 3 s start at the report, not the recall: Live's message thread can be busy for longer (saving the set), and the recall used to give way and the old song come back. [Nov 3, fourth review]
- The report to Live never overwrites a newer value: every change Live makes to a parameter (other than the plugin's own report) is counted, and a recalled value whose parameter has since moved away from its value at the recall is neither reported nor held. A knob turned (or a second recall) between the recall and the 30 Hz report used to be written back over with the first recall's value. Live echoing an old value doesn't count as a move. [Nov 3, third review]
- If the transport jumps back while a song waits for its bar line (an Arrangement loop wrapped, a relocate), that bar may never come: every plugin then recalls on its own next bar line, together, and if the transport jumps back again first (a loop with no bar line inside it) at that jump. A request made in the last block before a loop wraps reaches some plugins only after the wrap (SF CONTROL itself and the plugins Live processes before it); CONTROL sends where it was asked for, so such a plugin treats it as asked for before the jump, like the others, and a request asked for just after the wrap as a fresh one. The bar line counts as where the transport jumped from if it lies within a sample of it (a bar line between two samples can leave the loop's last block ending just past it). Before: such a request left CONTROL and the plugins before it waiting for good, and one asked for in the first block after a wrap landed a loop early in the plugins after CONTROL. [Nov 3, second review; wrap: third and fourth review]
- A song's KEY changes on the song's bar in every plugin: CONTROL sends the key with the song, and each plugin switches in its bar-line chunk, whichever side of CONTROL Live processes it on (KEY read once per block used to switch a block early or late). [Nov 3, second review]
- Safety: after every chunk, NaN/Inf become silence, the output is clamped at +12 dBFS, and NaN or 250 ms held at the clamp resets the module (FAULT, red, in the header).
- PANIC (SF CONTROL): every SF plugin fades out over 5 ms, then clears every voice, tail, delay line, envelope and filter state, and stays silent for as long as PANIC is held (effects take no input, instruments no new notes, DECK keeps its place in time); on release the input and the output both fade back in over 5 ms (so an effect with latency, DELAY's dry path or LIMIT, never plays a step). Use it for a runaway, a stuck note or a wrong song recall in front of a crowd. A second PANIC during the fade doesn't restart it. While it is held every SF plugin's header shows PANIC in red. Momentary buttons (PANIC, STORE ALL, THROW, RESET ...) never reopen pressed with a set, and a plugin window closed while one is held releases it, so PANIC can't be left latched. [held mute: Nov 3, second audit; release, latch: Nov 3, second review] PANIC also ends an in-set capture asked for before it, whatever stage it has reached, even one still waiting for SF CAPTURE: no sweep after the release, no take kept (SF CAPTURE) [93d84a4 audit, B01].
- No allocation, locks or file access on the audio thread. Samples are swapped through an atomic pointer; replaced samples are freed on the message thread after 2 s.

## Songs
Every plugin stores 16 song slots of its own parameter values (with the Live set). SF CONTROL selects the song (SONG knob/menu or Program Change); each plugin recalls its slot on the next bar. STORE ALL on SF CONTROL stores every plugin's current values into the selected song, or into the song STORE TO names (ACTIVE SONG = the selected song), which is not recalled: choosing a stored song on SONG first replaced the sound before it could be stored. The STORE TO song becomes the selected song (SONG follows, STORE TO goes back to ACTIVE SONG), unless a song change is under way: that change still lands and stays selected. SONG follows the bus (after a Program Change or STORE TO) only while the bus still has that song, and CONTROL never takes its own SONG update for a request: a Program Change right after STORE TO used to be overridden by it. STORE ALL goes out at the end of a host block in which no song recall landed: in such a block the plugins Live processes before CONTROL would store after the recall and those after it before (a mixed song, and with STORE ALL into the arriving song the plugins after CONTROL kept the old sound). STORE on any plugin stores just that plugin, into the selected song. [STORE TO: Nov 3, third review; the rest: fourth review] Empty slots leave the plugin as it is. Arrangement automation wins over recalled values (as on any device). SLOW applies to recalls, so a song change can glide. MOTION settings and SF CONTROL's KEY are part of songs. [Nov 5 audit, F2 F3 F8] The newest intent wins, the same in sound and storage: a recalled value holds (ovr) until Live reports it back, a change in Live is newer, or 3 s pass after the report. Once the recall is reported, any change in Live after the report is the performer's, the value from before the recall included (it used to count as an echo and was ignored for 3 s; until the report a return to the old value still can't be told from Live sending it again). One decision (overrideHolds) serves the sound and STORE: STORE (and STORE TO) stores the recalled value only while it is still in force, else the knob's (it used to store a recall that a newer move had replaced). The report's own check and write are two steps; a move landing between them is still taken as Live's own (a narrow window, no case seen). A Program Change for the song already in force cancels one still waiting (A, B, A before the next block: A stays; it used to land B); asking for the song in force is not a recall, so nothing set since is lost. [352df3c audit, A01 A06] A state saved while a recall is in force (Live not told yet) saves the recalled values, what sounds (overrideInForce, which asks without changing anything): it used to save Live's values from before the recall, so a set could reopen 6 dB off. A move away from the value Live had at the recall counts as the performer's even if later moves come back to it before Live reports (+3 then 0: the 0 stays; departGen, the recall in which the knob last left that value); it used to be read as Live sending the old value again. [93d84a4 audit, B02] A state loaded (setStateInformation: a preset from Live's browser, undo, a set opening) is newer than everything asked for before it, whatever its values. stateGen counts loads (odd while one is under way); a recall belongs to the count at its landing (ovrState), and one from before a load is no longer in force: overrideHolds drops it on the audio thread, overrideInForce (the save) and the report to Live skip it, so Live is never told it afterwards. It used to win whenever the state's value was Live's from before the recall: a state at 0 dB loaded over a recall of -6 not yet reported played -6, Live was then told -6 and the next save kept it (prepared again or not). The audio thread, seeing a new count, ends a song still waiting for its bar line and a STORE not yet made, and passes over SF CONTROL's song and store requests up to the counts at the load's end (songSeqAtLoad, storeSeqAtLoad); requests after the load go ahead as ever, and the knobs start where the state has them (no glide; this was set from the loading thread). Live loads a state on its message thread, where the report runs too, so a load never comes between the report's check and its write. The songs have one writer at a time (songLock): a STORE on the audio thread only tries it (taken: the next block), never runs while a load is under way (the load turns the count odd under the lock), and a state save copies the songs under it (it used to read them again until whole, up to 1000 times, and could give up). A preset from the plugin's own PRESET menu counts as a move on every parameter it sets (departGen), so a recall Live hasn't been told of never comes back over it. SFTests --authority2 (SF FILTER's LEVEL, song 1 at -6): a state at 0, -6 and +3 dB loaded over a recall of -6 from 0 that Live hasn't been told of; Live told before the load, between the load and the next block, or after it; prepared again or not: all 18 heard at once and settled, held by Live and saved as the state's (before: 4 of the 6 at 0 dB played and saved -6); a recall after the load; SLOW 1 BAR: a recall gliding when a state at +3 comes in gives way at once, one after it glides and a save keeps its target (A01); a move after the load survives the old report; a song waiting for its bar line and a STORE (the plugin's, STORE ALL) asked before the load are over, those after it land; STOREs every block while two other threads load and save states: no song part one state's and part another's.

## Presets [added Nov 3]
- The PRESET button in every header opens the menu: factory presets in GROUP submenus, user presets, SAVE USER PRESET, SHOW PRESET FOLDER. The button shows the loaded preset.
- A preset sets every sound parameter of the plugin (unnamed ones go to their defaults) and reports each change to Live, so the E16 rings follow. It never touches SLOW, MACRO SRC / AMT, ALLOC participation, ROLE, KICK BUS / MIDI sources, or venue data. Presets may set MOTION.
- 191 factory presets across 26 plugins (instruments, effects, master tools; none for DECK, CONTROL, ROOM EQ, ALLOC, TAP). Every one is rendered by SFTests: finite, in range, no runaway.
- User presets: one XML file per preset in `~/Music/SF Live Suite/Presets/<MODULE>/`. Copy the folder to move them between machines.

## MOTION [added Nov 3]
Two modulators in every plugin (M1, M2), stored with songs and presets.
- SOURCE: SINE, TRIANGLE, SQUARE, RANDOM (stepped), DRIFT (smooth random) are bipolar around the knob; RAMP UP / RAMP DOWN are one-way ramps that restart every RATE; KICK follows SF KICK's hits (RATE sets the release); NOTES (instruments: note velocity) or INPUT (effects: input level), RATE sets the release.
- RATE: 1/16 to 64 BAR, then (appended [Nov 4], so saved rates keep their index) 3/16, 5/16, 7/16, 1/4D, 1/2D, 1/4T, 1/2T, 3 BAR, 5 BAR, 7 BAR: cycles that run against the bar and meet it again every few bars (5/16 every 5, 7/16 every 7, the dotted values and 3 BAR every 3). All locked to Live's song position, so every plugin's modulators move together and move the same way every time the set is played. RANDOM and DRIFT are seeded per plugin instance; the seed is saved with the set.
- TARGET: any continuous parameter of the plugin. DEPTH: -100 to +100 % of the target's whole range, applied in the knob's own scale (a sweep of CUTOFF moves evenly in pitch, not in Hz). The modulation adds to the knob, MACRO and SLOW; the knob shows a bar for the modulated value.
- Steps (SQUARE, RANDOM, ramp resets, loop jumps) are slewed over 3 ms: no clicks.

## KEY [added Nov 3]
SF CONTROL's KEY (OFF, C ... B; stored per song) transposes the suite while SF CONTROL is running (the bus outlives sets, so a key left by a deleted CONTROL or another set is ignored). Parts are written in C; with KEY on, every pitched SF instrument plays them in the chosen key (nearest transposition, -6 to +5 semitones). KICK, RUMBLE and STRIKE snap their own pitch (the kick's fundamental, the rumble's thump, the object's main partial) to the nearest note of the key root, so the low end is always in tune with the track. KEY FOLLOW on each instrument opts out. OFF = no change anywhere.

## POCKET (kick-aware ducking)
SF KICK reports every hit to the bus: time (in beats), velocity, pitch sweep start/end and time constant, amplitude decay, and its FILTER corners. Any module with Pocket carves a kick-shaped hole in itself:
- DEPTH: how deep. SHAPE: one knob from transparent to pumping. Left: a narrow cut that follows the kick's pitch sweep down to its fundamental (felt, not heard). Middle: the low band (below ~3x the kick fundamental) ducks for the kick's length. Right: full-band pump, release tied to the tempo.
- Adapts to the kick: velocity scales each pocket (ghost kicks get shallow pockets), decay sets the length, the pitch sweep sets the frequencies, a high-passed kick closes the low pocket (the bass gets the sub), rolls merge into one dip on transparent settings and pump per hit on pumping settings.
- Safety: when kick tails overlap so much the pocket would be permanent, the pocket shrinks to the attack.
- Kick returns after a gap: pockets fade back in over a beat (breakdowns open up the bass automatically).
- Sidechain fallback (SF DUCK, SOURCE = SIDECHAIN, or any time no SF KICK is reporting): low-band envelope of the sidechain input.
- Transport jumps [decided Nov 2]: kick reports carry a wall-clock stamp; a report is used only if the wall time since it fits the musical time since it, so reports from before a loop wrap / restart / relocate are ignored. RUMBLE re-arms on a backward jump. POCKET OFFSET on SF CONTROL (+/-20 ms) calibrates Pocket and RUMBLE timing for the host if needed.
- Timing: Pocket runs 512 samples of lookahead (reported to Live as latency) so a kick report from the same audio cycle arrives before it is needed. Requires Live's buffer size <= 512. [decided Nov 2, needs Live test]
- After SF BUILD's drop [Nov 3, third review]: for DUCK TIME after the drop's bar line every Pocket stage also dips the whole band DROP DUCK dB under each kick, whatever its DEPTH (full for the first half of the window, easing out over the second; each dip follows the kick's own envelope, back within half a beat). Only the drop's own kicks count (from 10 ms before its bar line: MIDI timing), and the duck fades in over 5 ms from the bar line, so it never starts with a step (a roll into the drop used to start it at the depth the roll's last kick still had, a click on pads and bass). BUILD publishes the drop when DROP is pressed, before the bar; like a kick report, it is ignored after a jump back (the song position before where it was planned, or more wall time since then than song time). TAP and STRIP with ROLE KICK, PERC or DECK don't take part (SF KICK, PERC, STRIKE and DECK have no Pocket).

## ALLOC (mix assigner) [redesigned Nov 3]
Ten fixed bands (20-60, 60-100, 100-150, 150-220, 220-320, 320-480, 480-750, 750-1500, 1.5k-4k, 4k-20k Hz). Every instrument, SF STRIP, SF TAP and SF DECK measures its energy per band (40 ms attack, 80 ms release; the mean of both channels' energies, so wide and out-of-phase stereo counts) and publishes it; SF ALLOC decides cuts; each source applies its own cuts (bell EQ per band, gliding at 60 dB/s). The audio never leaves its track.
- A global order of sources (drag in SF ALLOC's list; top = most important), saved with the set, changed only by dragging. The order holds plugin instances, not names [Nov 3, second audit]: every SF plugin has an id saved with the set, so a source keeps its place when the set reopens (whatever order the plugins start in), when its track is renamed and next to sources of the same name (it used to go by name, and two PERCs listed as PERC / PERC 2 by start-up order could swap). A duplicated track's copy takes a new id and joins the end. Orders from older sets (names) are taken over on first sight. Any band can have its own order instead [decided Nov 3: different frequencies want different priorities]: click the band, drag (band marked OWN); LINK TO ALL returns it to the global order. Newcomers join the end of every order. Names: Live's track name (VST3 passes it; AU does not), else the module/role name; duplicates get " 2", " 3".
- Per band, a priority curve over the order: PRIORITY = position of the peak (0 top .. 100 % bottom), DEPTH = how hard sources away from the peak are cut (x MAX CUT; 0 = untouched), Q = width (Gaussian, sigma 3 rows at 0 .. 0.3 rows at 100 %). cut_i = MAX CUT x DEPTH x (1 - weight_i).
- MODE DYNAMIC (default): cut_i is scaled by how loudly the most active more-favoured source is playing in that band (above the SENSITIVITY floor, full at +18 dB). STATIC: always cut.
- Display (the window's strip, top to bottom): the order list (drag), ALL BANDS / LINK TO ALL, the ten bands side by side, each with one lane per source in its own order (white = what plays, amber = what is cut), the curve (grey; cyan while its knob is touched) and a dot for an own order, then a row each of PRIORITY, DEPTH and Q, a small knob per band. The 30 band knobs are hidden from the generic grid but automatable.
- Replaces the conflict-slot design (Nov 2), which was unpredictable: hidden role priority, load-order ties, and knobs that rebound to whichever conflict was worst.

# Modules

## Instruments (MIDI tracks)
Mono models (BASS, ACID, MONO, MS20, HOOVER, SYNC): DENSITY echoes never interrupt a note that is still held; they fill the gaps between notes.

Every instrument has a MODEL menu (DETAIL, first), a SAMPLE model (drop a file or LOAD; embedded in the set as FLAC), DENSITY echoes, GATE, bipolar FILTER, ALLOC participation (ALLOC toggle), a LEVEL, and KEY FOLLOW (follows SF CONTROL's KEY; on by default) [Nov 3]. Every model is calibrated to the role's loudness target (Gain staging).

The part of a sample that plays [Nov 6]: every sampler (KICK, PERC, RUMBLE, BASS, LEAD, PAD; VOICE's phrase) has START and END (0-100 % of the sample) and FADE IN after START and FADE OUT before END (0-2000 ms, raised cosine), appended to its parameters (smp_start, smp_end, smp_fade_in, smp_fade_out; the window's SAMPLE group). As an Ableton clip's start, end and fades: the module's own shaping (DECAY's gate, the amp envelope, VOICE's 3 ms slice fade) works on each note on top. The fades are in the sample's own time: played an octave up, they pass in half the time. A point inside the sample always fades for 1 ms at least, so a cut into a waveform never clicks; at the sample's own ends a fade of 0 is none. END is never before START (1 ms after it at least); fades longer than the part are scaled to fit it. A note takes the part as it is when it starts (a mono legato slide plays on); GRANULAR's grains come from the part as the knobs are now, so START moved while a pad holds moves where its grains come from (a part shorter than the grains makes them shorter, never none). Not set by presets (a preset carries no sample: choosing one leaves the part of yours that plays alone; user presets don't store them); kept in songs like any knob. At the defaults (0, 100 %, 0, 0) a sample plays as before these existed, to the bit (SampleRegion in Source/core/Sample.h; a value a hair off a default, as knob smoothing leaves it, counts as the default): 13 renders of every sample path (KICK main and layer, gated and not; PERC; RUMBLE; BASS, LEAD main and layer, PAD SAMPLE and GRANULAR; VOICE AUTO, EQUAL 8, WHOLE) identical to the bit before and after. On the display a press takes the nearer of START and END and drags it; near the sample's ends it snaps to them; a drag that leaves the display goes on (the editor keeps a drag begun on the display there: ALLOC's knobs too). The display grows by the strip when a sample loads (the editor's timer relays the window out when a module's display height changes). SFTests --region: the defaults; every sampler playing START 20 % .. END 40 % of a 1.5 s sample (its loud middle: 0.3 s of it, its start 5x louder than the whole's), FADE IN / OUT 100 ms (half the level 50 ms in and 50 ms before END), KICK an octave up (the part and its fades in half the time); START and END cut at waveform peaks: the steepest step 0.6-2.6 x the waveform's own (the sample's own start at a peak: 45 x); KICK's Pocket decay of a 75 ms part 0.075 s (the whole: 0.59 s); GRANULAR's grains from the part (a silent part: silence; 3.75 ms of the loud part: sound; moved while held: they follow); every factory preset of the samplers leaves the region; the editor's group, the strip appearing with a sample, and drags.

DENSITY (instruments): fills a sparse pattern with ghost notes. 0-50 %: one echo one ECHO SPACE later fades in (1/8 hats become 1/16 hats with ECHO SPACE 1/16). 50-100 %: two more ghosts at half spacing (1/32 rolls). Each generation is quieter, darker and shorter. ECHO SWING, ECHO PROB where useful. A ghost carries two levels [Nov 3, second audit]: its velocity (played softer: the instrument's velocity response, so no accents) and its DENSITY fade, applied after the velocity response, so a ghost really rises from silence (at 1 % of the knob it is 30 dB down; the velocity floors used to make it nearly full level). A quiet ghost (fade under half) doesn't cut the ringing kick or CHOKE the previous hit.

GATE (instruments): 0 = clipped staccato, 50 % = as played, 100 % = held long (release up to 8x).

### SF VOICE
Spoken-word / vocal phrases. A loaded phrase is sliced (SLICE MODE: AUTO onsets / EQUAL 8 / EQUAL 16 / WHOLE); C3 plays slice 1, C#3 slice 2 ...; keys below C3 play the whole phrase. START and END [Nov 6] set the part that is sliced (Slices in Source/core/Sample.h): AUTO takes the onsets between them, START itself starting the first slice unless an onset follows it within 40 ms (as the analysis does at the phrase's own start); EQUAL 8 / 16 divide START..END; WHOLE and keys below C3 play START..END; the last slice ends at END. ### SF STRIKE [added Nov 3]
Blast-excited resonant objects: industrial impacts, bells, sheet metal, distant detonations. Four voices ring at once: a fifth strike fades the quietest out over 5 ms (never cut); four more slots hold the fades. A pressure wave (Friedlander waveform: sharp overpressure, then a negative phase) from a charge of POWER at DISTANCE hits an OBJECT modelled as 6-16 resonant modes. The blast scales like the real thing: its duration grows with the cube root of the charge, its level falls with distance, and distance takes the highs away (air absorption). A short, close blast excites the high modes; a long, distant push the low ones. Big hits sound sharp for a moment and settle (TENSION). Above ~70 % POWER the object FRACTURES: modes split and detune, debris rattles, and the output saturates (2x oversampled; the amount follows the fractured voice's own envelope, smoothed, and fades in from clean, so nothing else ringing changes level).
- Objects: BELL, PLATE, BAR, TUBE, MEMBRANE, GONG (measured mode ratios).
- Row: POWER, DISTANCE, SIZE (pitch: bigger = lower), MATERIAL (wood to glass: how long the high modes ring), DECAY, DENSITY, FILTER, LEVEL. Also OBJECT, POSITION (strike point: mutes modes near a node), SPREAD (modes across the stereo field), TENSION, TUNE, KEY TRACK, FILTER RES, ECHO SPACE / SWING, VEL SENS (velocity scales the charge), ALLOC, KEY FOLLOW (the main partial lands on the nearest key root, TUNE moves from there; SIZE then picks the octave).
- Display: the blast's pressure-time curve for the current POWER / DISTANCE, and the object's modes.

### SF DECK
Two decks inside the set. Tracklist (drop files; persisted by path), background analysis on add/load: tempo + beat grid (onset regression, sample-accurate), integrated LUFS (BS.1770). Row: XFADE, FILTER A, FILTER B, LOW A, LOW B, ROLL, GATE (1/16 trance gate), LEVEL. Also PLAY A/B (start on the next QUANT boundary: 1 BEAT / 1 BAR / 4 / 8 / 16 BAR), MID/HIGH A/B (isolator: 0 = kill, 75 % = 0 dB, 100 % = +6 dB), KEY LOCK (Signalsmith Stretch, MIT licence; off = varispeed), CUE A/B (to the CUE output, pre-crossfader, for headphones), TRIM TARGET (every track normalised to this LUFS), FILTER RES.
- Decks always follow Live's tempo. A plugin cannot change Live's tempo; use Live's tempo automation for tempo transitions. [decided Nov 2]
- Hot cues from MIDI: C1-D#1 deck A cues 1-4, C2-D#2 deck B (jump on the next beat, short fade in; ignored while the deck is stopped). Default cues at beats 0 / 32 / 64 / 96.
- ROLL: slip loop roll (the track keeps running underneath; releasing returns to where it would have been). The rolled audio is read at tempo ratio, so with a large tempo difference it is slightly pitched (124 vs 128 BPM = about half a semitone).
- Loading a deck uses its own queue (never waits for list analysis) and reuses the list's analysis. Tracks are freed on the message thread, never on the audio thread.
- Transport jumps: playing decks keep running and are re-anchored by whole beats (stay on Live's grid). Beat grids are cached in seconds, so a sample-rate change keeps them valid. [decided Nov 2]
- Track log: every deck start is appended to Documents/SF Live Suite/tracklog.txt.
- Stems: use Live's stem separation, stems on their own tracks with SF TAP. [decided Nov 2]

Also MOD, WIDTH, GATED + GATE TIME, LO CUT, DUCK SHAPE, INPUT. 8-line feedback delay network with input diffusion and two-band absorption per line: DECAY sets the low-frequency RT60 exactly, DARK shortens the highs only [Nov 3].
- SF TERROIR [Nov 4]: convolution reverb for real places. SIZE, DECAY, DARK, PREDELAY, DUCK, FREEZE, THROW, MIX (SF REVERB's first eight: the same ids in the same places, so it swaps in for SF REVERB on B SPACE and the E16 addresses fit; layout.py ALTERNATES, check_set.py and E16Map.h accept it there). Also PLACE (slot 1-8, in songs, not in presets), LENGTH (FULL, 4 BAR-1/16), REVERSE, START (0-250 ms), WIDTH (0-200 %), LO CUT, DUCK SHAPE, INPUT, DRY.
  - Slots: each a capture stored in the set as FLAC (24-bit, its own rate), with name, kind, RT60 and range over the noise; an empty slot plays a built-in synthetic room (1.8 s, RT 1.1 s, no direct sound). Drop files on the plugin: one file to the selected slot (more to the following slots); two files named ... L / ... R (LEFT / RIGHT, any separator) make one stereo place: two takes (files of different lengths: nothing ties their clocks) are analysed one at a time and set side by side from their own onsets, each at the same energy; two files of one length are one recording split in two (an IR's L and R) and are analysed together, keeping the time between them. (Analysed together, two takes started by hand put the right side's response a second off.) A field recorder's poly file: the track names from its iXML TRACK_LIST (by INTERLEAVE_INDEX), else the sTRKn= lines of its bext description; tracks named MIX... (a MixPre writes MixL, MixR ahead of the inputs) are skipped when there are others, then the first two (one: a mono place); unnamed, the first two. Read in 64k-sample blocks, only the tracks used kept. Two channels carrying the same signal (difference 60 dB under it) make a mono place, its tail spread like any mono capture. Imports run on the module's worker thread; the status line reports the result (amber under 30 dB over the noise, red on a failure).
  - Captures (Source/core/IrTools): a recording holding the sweep is deconvolved (regularised spectral division, 30 Hz-18 kHz), every sweep found (spikes 40 dB over the median), the playback clock's drift fitted from their spacing and the reference regenerated at that pace (40 ppm measured to 0.1 ppm in the test), the responses averaged at their exact (fractional) positions, the speaker's harmonics left before them; a channel the sweep isn't in (its own spike 40 dB over its own median: a dead mic, an input not routed) is left out, the place mono from the other, and the status line says so. A clap recording (CLAP): the loudest event, from 1 ms before its first arrival (the earliest 0.5 ms up to 300 ms before the peak within 30 dB of the peak's and 20 dB over the floor, the quietest tenth before the peak: a finished IR's direct sound before a stronger reflection stays; silence is put in front when the file starts sooner, so a click at sample 0 is kept, not faded away) to where the smoothed 10 ms decay comes within 5 dB of the noise floor (the median of the last quarter when it is flat; none when it is still falling) and stays there 300 ms (a later arrival after a silent gap stays), cut before a second event (12 dB over the lowest level since, and within 6 dB of the first), the cut named on the status line (CUT AT x S: A SECOND CLAP) where the tail was still over the noise there. An IR file (IR FILE) is kept as it is [352df3c audit, A09: a reflection 0.8 as strong as the direct sound was cut as a second clap]: from 1 ms before its first moment 20 dB over the floor, however long before the peak and however weak, every later arrival and every gap, to two hops after its last 10 ms over the floor; only its last 10 ms fade out. A file shorter than 50 ms goes on in silence (a 5 ms IR is kept); one with nothing left, or with samples that are not numbers, is refused. What a file is, IMPORT says (AUTO, IR FILE, CLAP, SWEEP: how files come in, in no song or preset); SF CAPTURE's take is always the sweep (KnownSweep). [Nov 5 audit, F6] AUTO: every channel deconvolved, the evidence their energy together (a channel in opposite polarity used to cancel in the sum, and the sweep was taken for a clap); a sweep if there is a spike 40 dB over the median and it is broadband from its first moment (the 30 ms from 1 ms before the strongest: 250 Hz-4 kHz octaves hold 1 % of its energy, four of five within 30 dB) or comes back at the sweep's spacing (two repeats or more, within 0.4 % of whole periods: a narrow speaker or room still repeats, a click doesn't) [352df3c audit, A10]. A click in long silence deconvolves to a chirp, narrow at every instant, with a median near zero: it used to pass as SWEEP x1. Else an IR file if nothing is before its first arrival (under 5 ms of file, or under -95 dBFS: digital silence, a 16-bit file's dither; the first arrival is 1 % of the peak and 20 dB over the file's quietest 50 ms, so a noisy room isn't taken for its arrival), else a clap recording; the kind on the status line says which. The limit: one sweep through a narrow speaker is neither broadband nor repeated, and AUTO reads it as a clap; IMPORT SWEEP takes it (the spike only). Turns are CC Abs; hold presses are CC 127/0, toggle presses are notes (Live flips a switch on every Note On). The presses are in the file [Nov 3], in the encoding of a test scene exported from the OXI App: push type 2 = CC (nr1 = number, upper = P.Val, lower = R.Val), 1 = Note (nr1 = note, nr2 = velocity), 9 = Swap Destination, 0 = Off; mode 1 = Press & Release; output 0 = ALL, 15 = OFF (the App's list: ALL, TRS1, TRS2, USB1-3, BLE, Page, ALL -BLE, ALL -USB, OFF). Every press sends to ALL, like the turns. Knob 1 is a swap: LOW ENERGY, and with a quick press SLOW ALL on SF CONTROL (turn action 2, CC 34 ch 16, ring colour 2 green, 90); an encoder with a second destination and no swap would send both on every turn, so make_scene.py refuses one. Every E16 press is one the set plays through (DROP, THROW, FREEZE, ROLL, and the swap, which only chooses what knob 1 turns), because a knob is easily pressed while it is turned; nothing that would stop the set is on the E16. [Nov 7] The PA's emergency stop is the audio interface's output knob (the Babyface's, for the PA pair): PANIC and 92 PA no longer go on computer keys, and layout.py has no KEYS (PANIC stays on SF CONTROL, held in its window). Live: E16 Port 1 Remote in and out only (the port the E16 sends on; Port 2 before Nov 7), Takeover Mode Value Scaling. [E16 checks: a hold press held for 3 s stays on (a long E16 press also opens its own functions); a knob shared by several pages must not jump; if it does, `make_scene.py --relative-shared` sends those as CC Rel1 (Live: relative, Twos Complement).]

GLIDE RINGS [Nov 4]: on the E16, a knob that is travelling (SLOW) shows green and walks with the sound; when it arrives its ring is the knob's own again, and turning it shows the hand for 0.8 s. Every plugin finds its knobs in `Source/core/E16Map.h` (written by `make_scene.py` from layout.py: track, module, parameter index, address, the top of Live's mapping) by its Live track's name (VST3; Live reports name and colour in separate calls, and a call without the name must not wipe it [fixed Nov 4: SF ENERGY, PERC and LEAD, on several tracks, found no knobs]), or by module where the scene has that module on one track only, and every block reports its travelling knobs on the bus (a slot per plugin: up to 8 entries of channel, number and ring position, Live's mapping of where the sound is). SF CONTROL's timer (20 Hz, message thread) sends them to the E16 port chosen in E16 RINGS (OFF, PORT 1-3, default 3, which Live doesn't use) as SysEx F0 7D 53 46: kind 1 {channel-1, number, value/128, value%128} x 14 at most, kind 2 {channel-1, number} for arrivals; a knob is sent when it starts, when its ring moves 32 or more of 16383, and every 400 ms. The scene's script (controllers/e16/glide_rings.lua, minified into the scene by make_scene.py; its map leaves out a knob with a swap press, knob 1, which can't tell LOW ENERGY from SLOW ALL, so that ring is always the E16's own [Nov 5 audit]) paints those rings with leds.updateByIndex in colour 90 and gives them back (leds.reset) on an arrival, 1.2 s without a report, a page change or a turn of that knob. The knobs stay plain CC: without the script, SF CONTROL or the port, the rings are as before. Tests: SFTests --rings (lookup, ring values incl. a 0-70 % mapping, batching, keep-alive, arrivals, a removed plugin) and tests/glide_rings_test.py (the shipped script on a mock of the E16's Lua API, via lupa). [E16 check: a knob with SLOW ALL at 4 BAR walks green; CONTROL shows E16 RINGS PORT 3 in green.]

The show set is generated too [Nov 3]: `controllers/live/make_set.py` > `SF SHOW.als`, chapter 7's tracks, groups, returns, devices and routing with every address of `controllers/e16/layout.py` mapped. The OXI One's startup project [Nov 7]: `controllers/oxi-one`. `SFSTART.oxipro` is a project the ONE saved, with four scenes written into columns 1-4 by `make_start.py` (SPARSE, MAIN, VARIATION, EXIT: drums, the rolling bass and one stab, every note on the root; the other sequencers empty in all four, sequencer 8's PC off) and named SFSTART; every other byte is the ONE's. The .oxipro format is undocumented: `oxipro.py` holds what three saves of one project showed (the slot layout, CRC32 as OXI-Desktop's, mode, channel, Out, the Program Change, the tempo, the slot table, Mono and Multitrack step records, the drum tracks' channels), and a write is used only where it reproduces the ONE's own save byte for byte (README, HOW SFSTART WAS CHECKED). `check_project.py` reads any project against the card (exit 1 on a mismatch; a song's sequencer 8 must send its PC, PC n calling SONG n since the ONE stores n - 1).
